## Reuse and implementation boundaries

The current shared command/RPC/fence/oracle/ingress helpers are nested in the D2 controller. Extract only those needed by both D2 and D3; do not copy a second operational controller. Preserve D2 behavior and its completed journal. Keep one C authority lock across recovery and maintenance work.

Recovery has its own honest phase sequence (no fictional graceful quiesce): preflight/close ingress/fence/select cut/restore/compare/activate/baseline/route/verify, followed by rejoin-boot/rejoin/redundancy verification. Infer the two fixed phase plans from source/target: A→B is D2, B→A is D3; keep existing journal tables and preserve D2 evidence. Derive the current source epoch from completed writer authority plus exact route evidence, never the stale original installation epoch.

`hat recover A` may pause only after durable successful verification/routing. `hat rejoin B <operation>` continues only that exact operation and boundary, without allocating another writer epoch. The ingress gate must recognize this specific verified A-serving pause while rejecting earlier incomplete recovery. A rejoin failure preserves A's valid route and records the failed rejoin; it must not withdraw the healthy writer merely because maintenance failed. Conflicting new transitions still refuse until the operation is reconciled/completed. Preparatory boot/software installation on A remains an explicitly recorded, controller-locked setup action while B serves.

Node operations remain fixed, root-only and forced-command accessible, with exact configured epoch/current boot/operation binding. A fresh-recovery path may read a different explicitly approved source epoch into an isolated directory, but cannot reuse an old source DB, sidecar or uploader metadata. Activation still requires completed fencing, independent comparison and a reserved fresh epoch. Rejoin retains old data/config/metadata and starts only a cold standby with no activation authority.
